## What Vox does

1. **Pitch as a headline question.** Writers pitch "three ideas that all have headlines": a topic, an angle and the visual evidence they'll show. Animation ranks near the bottom of the list; the story comes first.
2. **Report first, then write.** They call experts or the people in the story, and write the script around what the reporting found.
3. **Write to the visuals.** A story editor holds the writer to one rule: "the whole point of video is that you're talking about the thing onscreen." While drafting each sentence, the writer names the image that proves it: a document, a chart, a map, a photo.
4. **"Look at this."** The narration points at the evidence ("Look at this map") instead of describing something off-screen.
5. **Delay the reveal.** Joss Fong held back the black-hole photo until the viewer understood why it was hard to take. The payoff comes after the tension.
6. **Leave room to breathe.** Conversational phrases ("That's right.", "Here's the thing.") give the listener processing time that reading doesn't need, so they stay in.
7. **One idea per scene.** Lines are short (about 20 words or fewer) so the voice never sounds rushed. The story editor cuts tangents, however interesting.
8. **Hook → context → evidence → takeaway**, repeated at chapter scale. Each chapter opens with a question the last one raised.
9. **Restraint in the edit.** Jump cuts instead of flashy transitions, which "convey no information". Hold a strong image for 5–6 s. Change the music about every 20 s, and use it to mark sections.
10. **Animated opinion essay.** No desks and no talking heads as the backbone. Motion graphics, archival material, maps and narration carry the story.
11. **Fairness.** Give the strongest version of the other side, then say what the evidence supports.

## Our script template

A scene table with **time | narration (≤ 20 words per line) | on screen (the evidence)**.
- **Cold open (≤ 60 s):** a specific place and a sensory detail, then the headline question.
- **Chapters of 1.5–3 min,** each with a title card. Each opens with a question and ends on a line that sets up the next.
- **Every number** gets a source, which is shown on screen in small type.
- **One "Look at this" moment per chapter,** on the key chart or map.
- **Ending:** zoom out from the specific case to the bigger idea, in a single memorable line.
