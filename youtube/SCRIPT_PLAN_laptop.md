# Script plan: "Why Your Laptop Gets Slower Every Year (It's Not Age)"

Planned with the user's guide **"How to Write a Million-View Explainer Script — Step by Step"** (Claude Docs, 1 Nov
2026). Each beat names the guide step it uses. Target: about 8–9 minutes, about 1,300–1,400 words at the guide's pace
(video 1: 1,449 words in 9:34). No hands-on testing: research and animation only (user's rule).

## Step 1: the question, before writing

| Item | Plan |
|---|---|
| Question with tension | Why does your laptop get slower every year, if the chip inside never changes? |
| Title (= the question) | Why Your Laptop Gets Slower Every Year (It's Not Age) |
| Thumbnail (= the feeling + 2–4 words, not the title) | The same laptop split down the middle, crisp "day one" vs cluttered "today", a frustrated face; text **SAME CHIP** |
| About me | Everyone has waited for an old laptop to wake up. |
| Surprising answer | The chip is as fast as on day one. The laptop got hot, its battery got tired, and above all the work it's asked to do got far heavier. |
| Drawable | Yes: one laptop, a cutaway of its insides, a then-vs-now split, counters and bars. |
| Common belief to break | "Laptops wear out, or the company slows them down on purpose." |
| One-sentence answer | Your laptop didn't get slower; it's carrying far more than it did on day one, and heat and an old battery make the load feel heavier. |

## Recurring pair (Step 4: a face that comes back)
The video has no presenter and no drawn people: the AI avatar appears only in the thumbnail, and the video is motion
graphics, typography, 2.5D/3D objects and effects (user, 2 Nov 2026). So the pair is two objects: **DAY ONE** (the
laptop new, glossy, instant) and **TODAY** (the same laptop, dusty, slow). The narration still speaks to "you".
The full script is in `SCRIPT_laptop.md` (about 5:50; the timings below were the first estimate).

Loops to track (Step 3): the big loop (0:40 → 6:55), one section loop per clue, and small loops at each guess
("How hot?", "How many replaced a battery?", "Twice? Ten times?"). All close except the grain of sand.
Re-hooks about every minute: the three guesses, the teasers at 2:40 / 4:55, the Apple twist at ~3:40, and picture
changes between eras.

## Hook draft (Step 2, the 5 moves), first 60 seconds
> It's nine-oh-two. You press the power button.
> The fan wakes up first. Then… nothing.
> A little circle spins. Your finger taps the desk. Your jaw goes tight.
> Now go back to day one.
> Same laptop. You open the lid, and it's ready before your coffee is.
> Same laptop. Same chip.
> So why was it instant then… and torture now?
> You've heard the answer. Computers wear out.
> Or the company slows them down on purpose.
> One of those turned out to be half true.
> And the real reason is much stranger.
> There are three things slowing your laptop down.
> The first one is hiding under your keyboard.

About 90 words: big question at word ~55, inside the guide's 50-second limit. No greeting, no channel name.

## Visual cues (Step 7), first scenes as the pattern
- **S01**: dark desk, 9:02 on a wall clock, old laptop lid opening. YOU, tired, one hand on the power button. Fan
  whir. On screen: "9:02".
- **S02**: close-up of the screen: a spinning loading circle. YOU's finger tapping the desk, jaw tight. Sound: the
  tap, the fan.
- **S03**: split screen. Left, labelled "DAY ONE": bright desk, new laptop, DAY-ONE YOU smiling, coffee still
  pouring. Right, "TODAY": the same laptop, YOU waiting. Sound: a soft chime on the left.
- **S04**: both halves freeze; the big question appears as a single line of text over the split.
- **S05**: cutaway of the laptop: keyboard lifts away to show the chip, the cooler, the fan. Teaser text: "CLUE 1".

Every later paragraph gets a cue the same way: scene number, place and time, characters and what their body does,
the emotion, on-screen text (only keywords and numbers), one sound.
