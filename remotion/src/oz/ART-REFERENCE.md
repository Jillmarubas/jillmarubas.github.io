# Oz ch. 1–2 "The Cyclone": Art-Direction Reference

Paper-cutout SVG style, real-world construction and proportions. Kansas is about 1890–1900.

**Master scale.** Adult woman (Aunt Em) = **5'6" = 66 in = 1 H**. Every size below is given in feet/inches and in H.
If an adult is drawn at **330 px** tall, then **1 ft = 60 px** and **1 in = 5 px**. Keep this scale for every element in the same depth plane.

**Book text to honour** (Gutenberg #55, https://www.gutenberg.org/files/55/55-h/55-h.htm):
- "Their house was small, for the lumber to build it had to be carried by wagon many miles. There were four walls, a floor and a roof, which made one room."
- The room held "a rusty looking cookstove, a cupboard for the dishes, a table, three or four chairs, and the beds". Henry and Em have "a big bed in one corner, and Dorothy a little bed in another corner".
- The cellar is "a small hole dug in the ground… reached by a trap door in the middle of the floor". **There is no outside cellar door.**
- The house "had been painted, but the sun blistered the paint and the rains washed it away, and now the house was as dull and gray as everything else".
- The prairie is "the great gray prairie on every side", and the sun "had baked the plowed land into a gray mass". There is no tree or house in sight.

## 1. The house: one-room frame homestead

**Real references.**
- A Hodgeman Co., KS homestead proof from 1879 lists a "framed house 12 by 22 ft, shingle roof, 2 doors, 4 windows" (NPS npgallery, https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/f3ba87bd-0888-4896-b03d-bef4febecdc6).
- Plains claim shanties were usually 10×12 to 12×14 ft (https://sdstate.edu/south-dakota-agricultural-heritage-museum/south-dakota-claim-shanty, https://library.ndsu.edu/exhibits/pioneer/name/panel4.htm).
- 12×14 ft is often quoted as the "proof" dwelling size (https://www.nps.gov/common/uploads/teachers/lessonplans/HomesteadActSummary.pdf). Two beds, a stove, a cupboard and a table need more room than that, so we size up.

## 2. Farm structures

**Barn / stable.** Henry has a modest farm, so draw a small frame stable or a sod stable.
- **Frame:** 16 × 24 ft with 10 ft walls and a 6:12 gable, unpainted vertical board-and-batten. Add a 4 ft hay door in the gable and a 4 × 7 ft plank stable door.
- **Sod:** walls **2–3 ft thick**, bricks cut about **12 × 24 × 4 in** and laid grass side down in staggered courses. Add a pole-and-brush roof under sod, and 1 small window set deep (https://www.kansasmemory.gov/category/11215 has Kansas sod-house photos).

**Windmill: Aermotor-type steel, 1890s.** Aermotor launched in 1888 and was selling about 20,000 mills a year by 1892. Its ads said an 8 ft steel mill outworked a 10 ft wooden one (https://www.gasenginemagazine.com/company-history/history-of-the-aermotor-windmill-corporation/).

| Part | Spec |
|---|---|
| Wheel | **8 ft diameter**, **18 curved galvanized steel sails** in a ring, about 16 in long, with 2 thin rim bands |
| Hub & gearing | A small cast gear box behind the wheel, with a pump rod running down the tower centre |
| Tail vane | A flat sheet about **4 ft long × 2 ft tall** on a 5–6 ft boom, behind the wheel. Leave off the brand lettering. |
| Tower | **4-post galvanized angle-steel**, **30 ft** (common range 20–40 ft; one reader's 1905 Aermotor stood on a 28 ft tower). It is about 6 ft square at the base, tapering to about 1.5 ft at the top. Cross-girts every 6–8 ft with diagonal X-braces. Add a small platform at the top. |
| Base | Legs on concrete or stone anchors. The pump and standpipe sit at the centre, with a spout running to the trough. |
| Colour | Galvanized zinc gray. It is the one slightly **bright, cool** metal in Kansas. |

- **Wooden alternative (Eclipse, Fairbanks-Morse type):** 10–12 ft solid wooden-slat wheel and a 4-post wooden tower with heavier timbers. It looks older and more rustic. Pick **one** type and keep it for the whole film.
- **Scale check:** the 30 ft tower is **5.5 H**, so the windmill hub rises about **2.4×** the house ridge.

**Fence.**
- Posts are **Osage-orange ("hedge") or cedar**, 5–6 in diameter, crooked and stubby, standing **4 ft** above grade. Space them **16.5 ft apart** (1 rod).
- String **3 strands** of 2-point barbed wire at about **15, 28 and 42 in**, sagging 1–2 in between posts.
- Draw barbs as tiny X ticks every **5 in**.

**Water trough.** Wooden plank or galvanized steel, **8 ft L × 2 ft W × 2 ft H**, at the foot of the windmill. Draw the water as a dark reflective strip.

**Wagon (farm/box wagon).**
- Box **10–12 ft long, 38–42 in wide, 2 ft deep**.
- Wheels: front **44 in**, rear **52–56 in**, with 12–14 spokes and iron tires.
- Add a tongue about 10 ft long and a spring seat.
- Period paint was a green box with red running gear. In Kansas grade it to faded gray-green and gray-red.

**Other props.** Henry runs "out to the sheds where the cows and horses were kept", so add a small cow/horse shed and a few chickens. Include a chopping block and a plough, plus a clothesline with 7 ft posts.

**Layout (wide establishing shot).** Put the house at centre-left and the windmill 40–60 ft behind it to the right. Place the stable 80–120 ft away, with fence lines receding to a dead-flat horizon. There are no trees.

## 3. Kansas landscape & sky

**Ground**
- Ploughed land: furrows run parallel at **about 12–14 in** spacing (sod-breaking plough). In perspective they converge on one vanishing point, so draw them as thin stripes that tighten toward the horizon.
- Prairie grass: short/mixed grass (buffalo grass, blue grama) **3–8 in**, with taller tufts of little bluestem **18–24 in** in the middle ground. Baum's grass is "not green… burned the tops of the long blades".
- Horizon: dead flat, placed at **1/3 frame height** for the wide shots. On flat land a 5'6" eye sees the horizon about 2.9 miles away, so it reads as a razor line with atmospheric haze.
- Atmosphere: values lighten and cool toward the horizon. Use 3–4 receding ground bands, each **8–12% lighter** than the one in front.

**Tornado: what it really looks like** (NOAA SPC Tornado FAQ, https://www.spc.noaa.gov/faq/tornado/)
- **Size:** most tornadoes are **"a few hundred yards across"**, and some exceed 1/4 mile. Use about **150–300 yd (450–900 ft)** at the ground. At 2 miles away that is about 2–5% of a 50 mm frame width.
- **Cloud base:** high-Plains storm bases sit about **3,000–5,000 ft** up. The funnel spans base to ground, so it is **4–8×** taller than it is wide.
- **Wall cloud:** a lowered, rotating block of cloud under the rain-free base, **0.5–2 mi wide**. The tornado hangs from it. It is darker than the base, with ragged scud wrapping around it.
- **Funnel shapes**
  - Cone: wide at the top, narrow at the ground.
  - Stovepipe: parallel sides.
  - Rope: thin and sinuous, usually in its dying stage.
- **Funnel colour** takes on the dirt it picks up. Kansas topsoil gives a gray-brown to tan funnel. Against a bright western sky it can backlight dark, or it can glow pale against a dark sky.
- **Debris cloud:** a rotating brown dust cloud at the base, **2–3× the funnel width**, with visible flecks (boards, shingles, hay). SPC notes it may show even when the funnel does not reach the ground.
- **Inflow:** dust blows *into* the storm along the ground, and the grass bows toward it ("the long grass bowed in waves", Baum).
- **Mammatus:** pouch-shaped lobes under the **anvil overhang**, not at the tornado. Rim-light them **warm (peach/copper)** when the sun is low behind the viewer.
- **Green sky** is real. A spectrophotometer study (Gallagher et al. 1996, BAMS "Green Thunderstorms Observed") recorded it. The leading explanation is low, reddened sunlight filtered through very deep, water-laden cloud (https://scicolloq.gsfc.nasa.gov/Bohren.htm, https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/fact-or-fiction-if-sky-is-green-run-for-cover-tornado-is-coming/). Keep it a **muted olive/teal-green**, not neon. It appears in the storm core and the hail shaft, while the sky elsewhere stays slate.
- **Light:** a late-afternoon low sun often slips under the western edge of the storm. This gives a yellowish **side-light** on the house and ground while the sky behind is nearly black. It is the most cinematic real tornado look.
- **Season and time:** Kansas tornadoes peak in **May–June, 4–8 pm**.

## 4. People & costume, circa 1900

**Body proportions.** Sources put the average adult at 7–7.5 heads (8 is the idealised canon) and children of about 7–12 at 6–6.5 heads (https://www.drawingforall.net/human-body-proportions/, https://anatomy4sculptors.com/?p=15).

| Character | Height | Heads tall | Head height (chin–crown) | Shoulder width | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aunt Em | 5'6" (66 in) | **7.5** | 8.8 in | 1.6 heads (14 in) | "thin and gaunt", narrow, slightly stooped |
| Uncle Henry | 5'9" (69 in) | **7.5** | 9.2 in | 2 heads (18 in) | lean, weathered, hands large |

| Witch of the North | about 4'4" | **6.5–7** | — | — | old woman: wrinkled, hair nearly white, "walked rather stiffly" |

**Proportion rules (no stick figures, even at 40 px tall).**
- Each figure is built from tapered limb shapes: upper arm, forearm and hand, then thigh, calf and foot. Limb width at the shoulder is about **0.4 head**, and at the wrist about **0.2 head**.
- The torso is a trapezoid of **3 heads** (chin to crotch is about 3 heads for adults and 2.5 for Dorothy).
- Legs make up **50%** of an adult's height and **47%** of Dorothy's. Fingertips hang to mid-thigh.
- In wide shots, keep **at least 5 shapes**: head, torso/dress, 2 legs (or a skirt hem with feet) and arms. Keep the head:body ratio exact.
- Elbows sit at the waist and the crotch at half height (adults).
- Dorothy's head is **0.98×** Aunt Em's in absolute size. Do not shrink her head.

**Dorothy (book + Denslow 1900).**
- Book: a "frock of gingham, with checks of white and blue", a "pink sunbonnet", and later the silver shoes and a little basket (ch. 3).
- Denslow's plates (public domain, https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/w-w-denslow-illustrations-wonderful-wizard-of-oz-1900/, scans at https://pdimagearchive.org) show a sturdy, round-faced little girl. She wears her hair in **two braids/pigtails** and a **knee-length** checked dress with puffed short sleeves and a round collar, with sturdy ankle shoes. Check details against the scans before finalising.
- Dress: 1890s girls' style. Fitted bodice, gathered full skirt to **just below the knee**, waist at the natural line, puffed short sleeves (summer), white Peter-Pan-type collar. Add black or white stockings and brown laced ankle boots.
- Gingham check: **½ in checks** (about 2.5 px at master scale). In SVG use a two-colour pattern of white ground, blue stripes in both directions, and a darker blue at the crossings.
- Pink sunbonnet: a deep, slat-stiffened brim **9–10 in** projecting past the face, a gathered crown, a **neck curtain ("bavolet") 6–8 in** down the back, and ties under the chin. The Kansas Historical Society calls the slat bonnet "the badge of the pioneer farm woman" (https://www.kansashistory.gov/teachers/trunks/pdfs/set_objcd_sunbonnet.pdf).

**Aunt Em.**
- Ankle-length **dark calico** work dress: small all-over print, long sleeves (1890s farm women wore long sleeves even in summer, https://unco.edu/hewit/doing-history/colorado-farmers-ranchers/food-clothing-shelter/clothing.aspx), a high band collar and a fitted bodice.
- A full-length **bib apron** in faded unbleached muslin.
- Hair parted in the middle and pulled into a **tight bun at the back**, gray-streaked brown.
- Face: sunken cheeks, thin lips; Baum says she "never smiled". Use plain black lace-up shoes.

**Uncle Henry.**
- **Long gray beard** to mid-chest (gray per the setting, and Denslow draws him bearded).
- Collarless band-neck **work shirt** in faded chambray or hickory stripe, sleeves rolled.
- **Leather or canvas suspenders (X- or Y-back)** and **brown duck or denim trousers** tucked into or over **mid-calf leather work boots**.
- A **sweat-stained wide-brim felt hat** with a 4 in crown dent and a 3 in brim.

**Munchkins (book).**
- "Round hats that rose to a small point a foot above their heads, with little bells around the brims."
- Men are "dressed in blue, of the same shade as their hats", with "well-polished boots with a deep roll of blue at the tops".
- The 3 men are bearded and elderly; give them faces "as old as Uncle Henry".
- Hat: a cone **12 in** above the head with a round brim of about **4 in**, and **8–12 small brass bells** hanging from it.
- Costume: a period-style fitted jacket and knee breeches or trousers, all in one blue. Boots are black with a turned-down cuff about **3 in** deep in blue.
- **Witch of the North:** white gown sprinkled with silver stars, a white pointed hat with bells, and a short wand-like stick.

**Toto.**
- "A little black dog, with long silky hair and small black eyes."
- **About 10–11 in at the shoulder, 15 in nose to tail-base**: roughly Dorothy's knee height.
- The long coat hides the legs and muzzle.
- For the silhouette, use a shaggy rounded body with a raised plume tail, small upright ears and a bright eye highlight.

## 5. Munchkin country

**Book.** "Lovely patches of greensward", "stately trees bearing rich and luscious fruits", "banks of gorgeous flowers", "birds with rare and brilliant plumage", and a brook "rushing and sparkling along between green banks". Ch. 3 adds round blue houses with big domed roofs, blue-painted fences, and fields of grain and vegetables.

**Real-world reference.** Base the scenery on a well-watered late-spring Midwest orchard and meadow, for example Missouri or Illinois in May.
- Trees: apple, pear and plum. Rounded crowns **15–25 ft** tall with **3–4 in** fruit.
- Flower banks: phlox, coreopsis, poppies, bee balm and asters, in drifts **1–3 ft** tall.
- Birds: real species for "brilliant plumage" are the cardinal, indigo bunting, painted bunting, scarlet tanager, goldfinch and Baltimore oriole. They are **5–8 in** long, so draw them small.

**Contrast plan: Kansas vs Oz.**
- **Kansas:**
  - Every colour sits between **HSL saturation 4–18%**, hue-shifted toward warm sepia gray (about 35–40° hue).
  - Value range is compressed: darkest about `#2A2826`, lightest about `#D8D4CB`, with no pure white.
  - Light is flat, overcast or white-hot, with soft shadows at **15–25%** darkening.
  - In SVG, either draw directly in the Kansas grade column above, or apply one `feColorMatrix type="saturate" values="0.15"` plus a sepia multiply of `#B5A68C` at 20%.
- **Storm:** the only colour allowed in Kansas is the **sickly olive-green storm cast** (saturation ≤25%), plus a low **yellow side-light** at 25–30%. These make the build-up feel ominous.
- **Flight:** the inside of the funnel is gray to near-black, with brown dust.
- **Landing:**
  - When Dorothy opens the door, colour arrives through the doorway first.
  - Then over **24–36 frames** (1–1.5 s at 24 fps) the filter saturation animates from 0.15 to 1.0 and the sepia overlay fades out.
  - Oz saturation targets are **55–90%**. Shadows go **cool blue**, not black, and highlights are clean white.
  - Value range is full: `#0F1E2A` to `#FFFFFF`.
- **Film note:** the broad idea of a monochrome-sepia Kansas giving way to full-colour Oz is a well-known mood device (the 1939 film used it). We borrow only that general concept. **Do not reference that film's character designs, costumes, sets, ruby slippers, Munchkinland architecture or yellow-brick spiral.** Baum's shoes are **silver**, and Munchkin houses are **round, blue and domed**, per the 1900 text.
- **Denslow note:** the 1900 first edition printed many in-text illustrations with tints keyed to each region (blue for the Munchkin chapters). This supports blue as the Munchkin dominant colour. Verify the tints on the scans.
