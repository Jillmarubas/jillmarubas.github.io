# The $20 Lie: Who Really Pays for Your AI

A documentary in the MagnatesMedia style for **Small Print**: narration only, nobody on camera, no experiments.

- **Draft 1**, for approval before production.
- **Narration:** about 1,150 words at about 165 words per minute, plus four 12-second music-only chapter cards. That comes to about **8½ minutes**.
- **Facts:** every number and quote is listed in `RESEARCH.md` with its source. Sources also appear on screen in small type. Items marked **check** there must be confirmed at the primary source before the voice-over is recorded.
- **Look (proposal):** cream thermal-receipt paper with red ink as the one accent colour. That ties into the channel name and the "receipt" idea that runs through the film. Grain, smoke and vignette on top; maps, documents and counters for the data.
- **People:** Sam Altman is quoted, not shown. If we show him, we use a licensed photo with parallax and no AI animation.

## Story map

- **Big question (opened in the cold open, answered at the end):** if AI keeps getting cheaper, why are you getting less for the same money?
- **Chapter 1** opens "the companies' bet" and ends on the cliffhanger *"wrong about how many words we'd want"*.
- **Chapter 2** opens the heavy users and ends on *"one company tried to fix that quietly… it did not go quietly"*.
- **Chapter 3** opens the price changes and ends on the OpenAI quote *"increasingly efficient models"*. That's the twist: viewers expect better models to mean more for their money.
- **Chapter 4** answers the big question (the inference paradox) and settles the title (the lie was the word *"unlimited"*).
- **Left open on purpose:** agents hiring other agents on your account. Who reads that small print?
