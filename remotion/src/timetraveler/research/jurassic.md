# Research: "How my ancestors survived the Jurassic"

Researched 24 Oct 2026, before any art. Each fact has its evidence level:
**Fossil** = directly from fossils · **Inferred** = from anatomy or close relatives ·
**Unknown** = artists' choice, so we pick something plausible and never claim it as fact.

## 1. The big catch: who were "my ancestors"?
- No humans, apes or even monkeys existed. Our ancestors in the Jurassic were tiny, furry,
  shrew-like mammals.
- **Juramaia sinensis** (China, about 160 million years ago) is the oldest known relative of placental
  mammals, the group that includes us. It was 7–10 cm long, weighed 13–17 g (about one AA
  battery) and ate insects. Its hands show it **climbed trees**, and the fossil keeps traces of **hair**. *Fossil.*
- **Morganucodon** (Early Jurassic) was mouse or shrew sized (about 10 cm, 27–80 g) and ate insects such as beetles.
  Its big eye sockets suggest it **came out at night** and spent the day in a burrow. *Inferred.*
- Comedy hook: the time traveler meets his great-great-…-grandparent, and it's a shrew
  hiding from dinosaurs. The survival plan is to be small, live in trees, come out at night
  and eat bugs.

## 2. Which dinosaurs (and which to avoid)
Jurassic = 201–145 million years ago. Many "Jurassic Park" animals are **not** Jurassic:
- **T. rex, Velociraptor, Triceratops, Spinosaurus** are all from the Cretaceous. *Fossil.*
- Joke: T. rex lived closer in time to us than to Stegosaurus (Stegosaurus about 150 million
  years ago, T. rex 68–66 million years ago).

Jurassic cast, with what's known:

| Animal | When / where | Skin and texture | Colour | Movement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Diplodocus** | about 150 Ma, Morrison (USA) | Polygonal scales of several kinds. *Fossil* (Gallagher et al. 2021, PeerJ) | Juveniles were **patterned or speckled, not plain grey**: the first melanosomes ever found in sauropod skin, including flat disc-shaped ones that in birds give bright or iridescent tones. *Fossil, but the exact hues are unknown* (Gallagher et al., Royal Society Open Science) | Tail held **off the ground** (trackways show almost no tail drag). Slow walk on pillar-like legs. |
| **Stegosaurus** (relative *Hesperosaurus*) | about 150 Ma, Morrison | Small polygonal scales broken up by larger oval bumps in **rosettes**. The plates were covered in **smooth keratin with long parallel grooves**. *Fossil* (Christiansen & Tschopp 2010) | Unknown | Low head, short front legs. Swung its spiked tail ("thagomizer") as a weapon: an Allosaurus bone with a healed puncture wound fits a spike, and about 10% of spikes have broken tips. *Fossil.* |
| **Allosaurus** | about 155–145 Ma, Morrison | Leathery skin of tiny **1–2 mm pebbly scales** with a few larger ovals up to 3 mm, "like chicken feet or a crocodile". *Fossil* (Hendrickx et al. 2022) | Unknown | Body level and balanced at the hips, tail stiff and off the ground. Hands faced **inward** (palms facing each other, not "bunny hands"). Tail swings slightly side to side when walking (a 2021 simulation of a related theropod). |
| **Dilophosaurus** | about 183 Ma, Early Jurassic, Arizona | Twin head crests, strong jaws, a real top predator. **No neck frill and no venom spit**: those were invented for the movie. *Fossil* (Marsh & Rowe 2020) | Unknown | Two-legged hunter. |
| **Archaeopteryx** | about 150 Ma, Germany | Feathered, with wings and a long bony tail. *Fossil* | Wing feather **mostly black**. *Fossil* (Carney et al. 2012) | Flew, at least short distances. |
| **Anchiornis** | about 160 Ma, China | Fully feathered, including its legs. *Fossil* | **Grey and black body, black-and-white wings, reddish crest**. *Fossil* (melanosomes; one later specimen showed only grey and black) | Small, birdlike. |
| **Kulindadromeus** | Middle–Late Jurassic, Siberia | A plant-eater with **both** fuzzy protofeathers and scales: hexagonal scales on the shins, small round ones on the hands and feet. *Fossil* | Unknown | Small two-legged runner. |
| **Rhamphorhynchus** (pterosaur, not a dinosaur) | about 150 Ma, Germany | Membrane wings, body covered in **fuzz (pycnofibers)**, long tail ending in a vane. *Fossil* | Unknown | Flew over lagoons, catching fish. |

Colour rule for the unknowns: use **countershading** (dark back, light belly), the pattern found
in every dinosaur whose colour pattern has been measured (*Sinosauropteryx*, *Borealopelta*,
*Psittacosaurus*), with muted earthy greens, browns and greys. Save bright colour for
display parts (crests, plates, feathers).

## 3. The world (backgrounds)
- **No grass and no flowers** anywhere. Fossil grass and flowers only appear in the
  Cretaceous. *Fossil* (NPS). So there are no green meadows: the ground cover is ferns, or bare soil.
- Plants of the Morrison Formation: **tall conifers** (monkey-puzzle-like *Araucaria*), **ginkgo**,
  **cycads** and cycadeoids (squat palm-like trunks), **tree ferns**, **horsetails**, and ferns as
  the understory. *Fossil.*
- Climate: warm and frost-free but **seasonally dry**, savannah-like fern plains with rivers,
  lakes and wetlands, and forests near water. There were severe droughts followed by floods. *Fossil, but the sources disagree on how forested it was.*
- Colour script idea: the green wet season (lush ferns by the river) for the calm parts; the
  dry season (ochre soil, brown ferns, dust) for the tense parts.

## 4. What "exact replica" can mean in our style
- **Exact:** body proportions, skeleton pose, scale patterns (rosettes, pebbles,
  polygons), feathers or fuzz where known, known colours (Anchiornis, Archaeopteryx),
  how they walked, and the plants.
- **Can't be exact:** the colours of most dinosaurs (Allosaurus, Stegosaurus, Diplodocus
  adults). Nobody knows them. We choose plausible, countershaded colours and never claim
  them as fact. On-screen wording: "scientists think…", "artist's guess".
- **Drawing style:** a photoreal skin texture would clash with a simple cartoon character.
  Keep the flat cartoon style, but put the real scale patterns in as line and dot texture drawn in
  VectorCraft.
